🎭 HEDGING LANGUAGE: THE DIPLOMAT'S TOOLKIT

💡 "To hedge is not to hide — it is to be honest about the limits of what you know."

🎧 1. Listening Activity · Hedging Tone

🎯Focus
Notice how the speaker sounds professional and honest, not weak or uncertain.
📝Listening Text
"The preliminary data tends to indicate a possible improvement in output... It would appear that the revised method may offer certain advantages... However, there seems to be some inconsistency in the results across different test conditions... This could potentially be attributed to variations in the ambient environment..."
🎯Task
Rewrite 3 overconfident statements using hedging language and record them.

🧠 Quick Listening Check

1. What does the speaker say about 'variation'?

2. What might be causing the issue?

🗣️ 2. Speaking Activity · The Academic Presentation

In research, you never overstate your conclusions. Hedging language shows intellectual integrity.

1
"The data appears to indicate that the composite material exhibits superior thermal resistance."
2
"It would seem that further investigation is warranted before a definitive conclusion can be drawn."
3
"This finding may well have significant implications for the design of future structures."
4
"There appears to be a degree of correlation between increased load and micro-fracture formation."
5
"To some extent, the results are consistent with the theoretical predictions of the model."
🎙️Your Academic Presentation Recording
👉WHY THIS WORKS: Overconfident language in technical reports creates legal and professional liability. Hedging is a sign of rigour, not weakness.

💡IDIOM OF THE DAY: "KEEP YOUR OPTIONS OPEN"

Marathi Meaning: पर्याय खुले ठेवणे

Meaning: To avoid making a final decision so you can change later

Example: "Until we receive the full test results, I'd rather keep our options open on the material selection."

🔥POWER WORD: TENTATIVE (TEN-tuh-tiv)

Meaning: Not certain or confirmed; provisional

Usage: "We have reached a tentative agreement on the timeline, pending approval from the head office."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Hedging Language — The 5 Tools

1. MODAL VERBS: may / might / could / would / should — "This could indicate a structural weakness."

2. ADVERBS: apparently / seemingly / presumably / arguably / possibly / perhaps — "The results are arguably the most significant finding."

3. VERBS: appear to / seem to / tend to / suggest / indicate — "The data appears to suggest a correlation."

4. NOUNS/ADJECTIVES: a tendency / a degree of / some evidence / certain limitations — "There is a tendency for performance to decrease."

5. QUALIFYING PHRASES: to some extent / in many cases / under certain conditions / in general — "To some extent, the theory is supported."
