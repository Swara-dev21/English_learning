🗣️ 2. Speaking Activity · The Structured Argument

A structured argument follows a clear pattern. Practice each phase of the argument structure.

1
THESIS: "It is my contention that communication skills should carry equal weight with technical competence in engineering education."
2
ARGUMENT 1: "To begin with, engineers who cannot articulate their ideas clearly risk project failures, regardless of their technical expertise."
3
COUNTER-ARGUMENT: "It could be argued that technical precision is the engineer's primary responsibility, and communication is secondary."
4
CONCESSION + REBUTTAL: "While this is a reasonable position, evidence from industry surveys consistently shows that poor communication is cited as the leading cause of project delays."
5
CONCLUSION: "On balance, a holistic approach that integrates both technical and communicative competence is not only desirable but essential."

📖 3. Reading Activity · The Policy Debate

The question of whether diploma engineers should receive mandatory communication training has generated considerable debate. Advocates maintain that technical knowledge alone is insufficient in a globalised workplace. They point to studies indicating that 70% of project failures can be attributed to communication breakdowns. Opponents, however, argue that adding compulsory communication modules would reduce time available for core engineering content. A pragmatic resolution might be to integrate communication skills into existing technical modules rather than treating them as separate subjects. This approach would, in theory, address both concerns without extending the duration of the programme.

💡IDIOM OF THE DAY: "PLAY DEVIL'S ADVOCATE"

Marathi Meaning: विरुद्ध बाजूचा मुद्दा मांडणे

Meaning: To argue a position you don't necessarily believe, to test an argument

Example: "Let me play devil's advocate for a moment: what if the data actually supports the opposite conclusion?"

🔥POWER WORD: JUXTAPOSE (JUK-stuh-pohz)

Meaning: To place two contrasting things together for comparison

Usage: "The report juxtaposes the high initial cost of renewable energy with the long-term savings it generates."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Essay Structure — The 5-Part Argument Framework

📖 INTRODUCTION:

• Hook: Start with a striking fact, question, or statement.

• Context: Provide brief background.

• Thesis: State your position clearly.

📖 BODY — ARGUMENT 1 (strongest):

• Topic sentence + Evidence + Analysis + Link back to thesis.

📖 BODY — ARGUMENT 2 or COUNTER-ARGUMENT:

• Acknowledge the opposing view fairly.

• Rebuttal: Show why your position is stronger.

📖 CONCLUSION:

• Restate thesis (in different words).

• Summarise key arguments.

• Broader implication or call to action.

📖 Useful Argument Phrases:

Thesis: 'It is my contention that...' / 'I would argue that...' / 'This essay will demonstrate that...'
Evidence: 'Studies indicate that...' / 'Research by [X] suggests...' / 'According to...'
Concession: 'While it is true that...' / 'Admittedly...'
Conclusion: 'In conclusion...' / 'On balance...' / 'Ultimately...'
