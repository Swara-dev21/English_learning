🗣️ 2. Speaking Activity · The "Technical Briefing"

When an engineer or a senior gives you instructions, you must ensure you have the correct data

🎯The Task
Imagine a teacher or a clerk just gave you a very fast explanation about a form or a lab experiment.
🗣️
The Exercise
Speak these lines out loud. Focus on your intonation—keep it professional:
1
"I apologize, but I didn't quite grasp the last point." (Smart substitute for understand)
2
"Could you please elaborate on the safety procedures?" (Smart substitute for give more details)
3
"To ensure I have this correct, you want me to... [insert actione e.g submit the form tommorrow]?"
4
"I appreciate your patience while I verify these details."
5
"Could you provide a brief summary of the main requirements?"
💡 WHY THIS WORKS

These phrases show that you are diligent. It is a sign of high intelligence to ask for clarification, not a sign of weakness.

Example: "Driving was hard at first, but now I'm getting the hang of it."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Simple Past vs. Present Perfect

🔹 The Hack: This is the most important choice in professional English!

Simple Past: Finished time (yesterday, in 2023, last week)

I finished the report yesterday.

Present Perfect: Unfinished time / Life experience (so far, today, already, yet)

I have finished 3 reports so far.

The Choice Rule: If you say WHEN it happened (yesterday, at 5 PM), use Simple Past. If the time is NOT mentioned or is still happening (today), use Present Perfect.
