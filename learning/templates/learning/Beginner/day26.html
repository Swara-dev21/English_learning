🔥POWER WORD: "INQUISITIVE"

Pronunciation: In-KWIZ-i-tiv

Meaning: Having or showing an interest in learning things; curious.

Usage: "An inquisitive engineer often discovers the root cause faster."

💬Idiom of the Day: "Elephant in the room"

Marathi Meaning: एखादी मोठी समस्या किंवा विषय ज्याकडे प्रत्येकजण सोयीस्करपणे दुर्लक्ष करत आहे.

The Logic : In a high-level meeting, there is often a glaring (obvious) problem that everyone is aware of, but no one wants to mention because it’s uncomfortable. A true leader has the equanimity to address the "elephant" and rectify the situation.

Example : "We discussed the new office décor for an hour, but the elephant in the room is that our main server has been down for two days."

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: The "Inversion" for Formal Questions

🔹 The Hack:To sound more professional and "Executive," use Inversion (flipping the subject and verb) or Indirect Questions. Indian speakers often use "Why you are..." when it should be "Why are you..."

1. Instead of "Why is the project late?" (Aggressive):

"To what do we attribute the delay?" (Inverted Professional)

2. Indirect (Polite) Questions:

"I was wondering if you could elucidate..."

"Would it be possible to peruse the data?"

"I would be interested to know your thoughts on..."

✍️ Activity: Turn these "Aggressive" questions into "Executive" inquiries

1. "What do you want?"

2. "When will you finish?"

3. "Why did this fail?"
