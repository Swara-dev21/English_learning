🗣️ 2. Speaking Activity : The "Exam Stress" Dialogue

In college, the most common conversation is about the pressure of submissions and examinations.
🎯The Task
Imagine you are talking to a friend outside the library. Both of you are worried about the upcoming Semester Exams.
🗣️
The Exercise
Speak these lines out loud. Try to sound authentic and supportive:
1
Friend: "Are you ready for the Viva tomorrow?"
2
You: "To be honest, I had neglected my notes until last week."
3
Friend: "Me too. I had assumed the exam was next month."
4
You: "I am striving to cover the main chapters tonight."
5
Friend: "It feels like a strenuous task, doesn't it?"
6
You: "Yes, but we have overcome difficult subjects before. Let's collaborate."
💡 WHY THIS WORKS

This uses Emotional Intelligence. Sharing a "Common Struggle" in English builds a stronger bond with your peers than just talking about work.

🔥POWER WORD OF THE DAY: "PERSEVERANCE"

Pronunciation: /pɜːsɪˈvɪərəns/ (Pur-se-VEER-ance)

Meaning: Persistence in doing something despite difficulty or delay in achieving success.

Usage: "His perseverance during the exam season was truly commendable."

💡Idiom of the Day: "Back to the drawing board"

Meaning: नवीन सुरुवात करणे (जेव्हा एखादी योजना अपयशी ठरते).

⚙️ The Logic: If your first attempt at a project fails, you don't quit; you go "back to the drawing board" to start a better plan.

Example: "Our first prototype failed, so it's back to the drawing board."

🧠 6. Grammar Hack: Past Perfect (The "Past of the Past")

Use this to show which of two past actions happened first.

Formula: Subject + Had + V3 (Past Participle)


Example: The train had left (1st action) before I arrived (2nd action) at the station.

Why use it?
It helps your listener understand the sequence of events. It makes your storytelling much clearer.
