📖 3. Reading Activity : Decoding Intent

Passage :
Advanced listeners do not simply process words — they decode intent. Every spoken message has three layers: the literal content (what was said), the emotional subtext (how it was said), and the strategic intent (why it was said at this moment). Most people hear only the first layer. Professionals trained in active listening hear all three simultaneously. In a technical environment, this skill separates the analyst from the engineer, and the engineer from the leader.

1️⃣ Name the three layers of every spoken message.

2️⃣ What does the author claim distinguishes a leader from an engineer, according to this passage?

3️⃣ Think of a recent conversation — which layer did you engage with?

🧠 6. Grammar Hack : Misuse of 'Doubt'

In Indian English, 'doubt' is often used to mean 'question' or 'query'. In standard English, doubt implies disbelief. Review the examples:


I have a doubt about the formula.  →  I have a question about the formula.

Please clear my doubt.  →  Please clarify my query.

Any doubts?  →  Any questions? / Does anyone need clarification?

🔷 Activity: Correct these sentences — replace 'doubt' with the proper word:


1. "I have a doubt regarding the project deadline."


2. "She raised a doubt during the meeting."


3. "Please clear my doubts about the procedure."
