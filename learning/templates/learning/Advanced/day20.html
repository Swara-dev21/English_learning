💡 TIP OF THE DAY

Critical thinking is not about being negative — it is about being precise. When you analyse any argument, use the four-question filter: (1) What is the claim? (2) What is the evidence? (3) Is the evidence sufficient and credible? (4) What is missing? Apply these four questions to every professional proposal, report, or presentation you encounter this week.

📖 3. Reading · Linguistic Awareness

Critical thinking and language are inseparable. The words we use to describe a problem shape the solutions we consider. A factory described as 'having recurring maintenance issues' invites reactive solutions. The same factory described as 'operating without a preventive maintenance framework' invites systemic redesign. Advanced communicators choose the language that opens the most productive solution space. They recognise when others use language to close down possibilities, and they re-frame the problem. This linguistic awareness is not manipulation — it is leadership.

(a) Explain how the two descriptions of maintenance issues shape different responses.

(b) Give an example from your field where problem-language determines the solution.

(c) Rewrite this limiting statement into a solution-oriented frame: 'Our project is always delayed.'

🔥POWER WORD: INCISIVE

Meaning: Intelligently analytical and clear-thinking

Usage: "Her incisive analysis of the failure report saved the production timeline."

💬IDIOM: "The bottom line"

Meaning: The most important point or conclusion

Example: "The bottom line is that without additional funding, the project cannot be completed on time."

🧠 Grammar Hack: Avoid 'Obviously' and 'Clearly'

These words assume the reader agrees — which can be offensive if they do not. Replace them with the evidence that makes the point true.

❌ Obviously, solar energy is the best solution. → ✅ Data from comparable installations indicates that solar energy delivers the highest ROI.

❌ Clearly, the project needs more time. → ✅ The current timeline does not allow for the three required regulatory approval stages.
