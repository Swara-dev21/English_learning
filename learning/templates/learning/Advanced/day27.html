💬 "The first draft shows thinking. The edited draft shows professionalism."

TIP OF THE DAY

Professional editors use the 'fresh eye' rule: never proofread immediately after writing. Allow at least 30 minutes before rereading your own work. Your brain remembers what it intended to write and will 'see' correct text even when errors exist. The fresh eye catches what the writing brain misses.

📖 3. Reading Activity : The Art of Professional Editing

Passage :
Professional editing is not about making writing shorter — it is about making every word earn its place. The three most common targets in a professional edit are: redundancy ('end result' → 'result'), nominalisation ('make a decision' → 'decide'), and hedging overload ('it might perhaps be possible that' → 'this may indicate'). Beyond these word-level corrections, advanced editors also check paragraph-level coherence. The most revealing question an editor can ask of any paragraph is: 'If I removed this paragraph, would the reader notice anything was missing?' If the answer is no, the paragraph should go.

3️⃣ Edit this sentence using all three techniques:

🧠 6. Grammar Hack : Always Read Your Writing Aloud

Your Ears Catch What Your Eyes Miss : Silent reading activates the same visual prediction that caused the error.
Reading aloud bypasses this and catches:


• Missing words: "The system failed at [missing: the] component level."

• Word repetition: "The the result was..."

• Wrong tense: "Yesterday, the team completes the test." → completed

• Unclear pronouns: "He told him that his report was wrong" — who told whom?

Rule: Read every professional document aloud at least once before submitting.
