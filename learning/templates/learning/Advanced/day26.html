TIP OF THE DAY

The golden rule of idiom use: never use an idiom you have only read, not heard. Before using any idiom professionally, find at least two examples of it used in that same context online or in a podcast. Idioms misused in professional settings can undermine your credibility faster than using no idioms at all.

📖 3. Reading Activity : The Role of Idioms

Passage :
Idioms are not decoration — in professional English, they are signals of cultural fluency and relational warmth. A speaker who uses idioms accurately signals that they have not just studied English but lived in it — absorbed it through extensive exposure. However, idioms carry a risk that vocabulary does not: they can be completely opaque to non-native listeners. In a multicultural engineering team, 'I think we are flogging a dead horse here' might leave three of your colleagues completely lost. Advanced communicators develop contextual judgement: they use idioms freely with native speakers in informal registers, reduce them in multicultural professional settings, and eliminate them entirely in technical documentation. The idiom, like all advanced language, is a tool — and tools are used when they serve the purpose.

🧠 6. Grammar Hack : Use Idioms Only When They Fit Naturally

Forced idiom use signals that you are trying to sound fluent rather than being fluent. Review:


❌ Regarding the project status, the ball is in your court in terms of moving forward with the next steps.

✅ The next steps are yours to initiate — I am ready to support as needed.

Rule: Read the sentence without the idiom. If it sounds better or equally good, do not use the idiom.
Idioms should add warmth or brevity, not complexity.
